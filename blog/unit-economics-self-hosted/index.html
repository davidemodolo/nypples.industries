The Unit Economics of Self-Hosted Inference: 20x Ascend vs. Hyperscaler GPU Rental.

Business May 30, 2026 · 13 min read

At €1M capex for 20 nodes, our cost per GPU-hour is €0.19 over 5 years — compared to €1.80–2.50/hr for equivalent cloud H100 instances. Here’s the full TCO model, from hardware depreciation to Italian industrial electricity pricing.

This isn’t a subsidy — it reflects Huawei’s strategy to build market share in Europe. Whether this pricing holds at scale is an open question, but for a 20-node deployment, it’s real.

Per-GPU-hour breakdown

We amortize hardware over 5 years (60 months), which is conservative for datacenter GPUs running at stable temperatures. The calculation:

  • Hardware depreciation: €820K hardware cost / (160 GPUs × 43,800 hours over 5 years) = €0.117/GPU-hr
  • Colocation: €4,500/month rent, power, cooling / (160 GPUs × 730 hours/month) = €0.039/GPU-hr
  • Power: 12 kW per rack at €0.18/kWh (Italian industrial rate) = €0.027/GPU-hr
  • Total cost per GPU-hour: €0.183

The break-even point

With monthly operating costs of approximately €29K (colo, power, 3-person team salaries, insurance, software licenses), our break-even is roughly €45K/month in revenue. At current pricing, that requires:

  • 25% GPU utilization at average blended pricing of €1.10 per million tokens (input + output)
  • Or approximately 15 active customers on the Startup tier (€2,990/month)
  • Or one enterprise contract with dedicated capacity

25% utilization is conservative — most cloud GPU providers target 50-70%. The difference is that we’re not trying to maximize utilization. We want headroom for burst capacity. A customer who gets throttled during a traffic spike doesn’t care about our utilization rate.

The cost of GDPR compliance

Self-hosting carries compliance costs that cloud rental avoids: physical security (biometric access, 24/7 monitoring), ISO 27001 certification maintenance, and dedicated DPO time. We budget approximately €2,500/month for compliance overhead. This is a real cost that cloud providers spread across millions of customers. For a 20-node deployment, it’s noticeable but not prohibitive.

The counterpoint: cloud GPU rental doesn’t solve GDPR. You still need DPAs, sub-processor documentation, and data transfer impact assessments. The compliance cost isn’t avoided — it’s just different. We’d argue that physical control makes compliance simpler, not harder.

Huawei Cloud Europe comparison

Huawei offers Ascend instances through Huawei Cloud Europe, with a Dublin region. Pricing is approximately €0.95/hr for equivalent Ascend 910C capacity — roughly 5× our cost but still competitive with H100 cloud pricing. The Dublin region is in the EU, satisfying data residency requirements. So why self-host?

Three reasons:

  1. Control. We can deploy models the day weights are released, not when Huawei Cloud adds them to their catalog.
  2. Margin. At €0.18/GPU-hr cost vs. €0.95/GPU-hr rental, we have room to price competitively while maintaining healthy margins.
  3. Data isolation. Multi-tenant cloud instances add a theoretical risk of cross-tenant data leakage. Our single-tenant deployment eliminates this entirely.

Is this sustainable?

The economics work at 20 nodes. The question is whether they work at 100 nodes, or 500 nodes. At larger scale, you need dedicated datacenter space (not colocation), redundant power, on-site engineering staff, and significantly more networking complexity.

Our bet is that the European market for sovereign AI inference is large enough to justify that scale. And at every scale, owning the hardware beats renting it — as long as you can keep utilization above 25%.
